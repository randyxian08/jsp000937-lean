# Low-cardinality checks against the displayed Erdős objectives

These calculations use exactly the displayed objective in [Erdős 1129](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1129) and [Erdős 1130](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1130): distinct interpolation nodes may be anywhere in `[-1,1]`. They explain why informal uniqueness or equal-peak remarks cannot be inserted as unconditional hypotheses into the unrestricted formal targets.

For 1129, take three nodes `(-a, 0, a)` with `0 < a ≤ 1`. Directly from the Lagrange products, writing `r = |t|/a`, the Lebesgue function is `1 + r - r²` for `|t| ≤ a` and `2r² - 1` for `a ≤ |t| ≤ 1`. Therefore its maximum on `[-1,1]` is `max(5/4, 2/a² - 1)`. At `a = 19/20`, the exterior value is `439/361 < 5/4`, so the maximum is `5/4`. At `a = 1`, the canonical node set `(-1,0,1)` also has maximum `5/4`; the 1129 page explicitly identifies this as the `n=3` minimum. Thus the literal unrestricted optimization has at least these two distinct minimizers. The submitted 936 target characterizes the free-node minimizers by reference shape **and** exterior inequalities; it does not assume uniqueness of an arbitrary unrestricted minimizer.

For 1130, take any two distinct nodes `u < v` in `[-1,1]`. On the internal interval `[u,v]`, the two cardinal functions are nonnegative and sum to one, so the internal peak is exactly one. Each exterior interval contains an interpolation node, so its peak is at least one. Thus the minimum of the three interval peaks is exactly one for **every** two-node family, making every such family a maximizer. For the particular pair `(-1/2,1/2)`, the two exterior peaks are two while the internal peak is one. Hence maximizing the literal all-gap minimum does not force all three peaks to be equal in this boundary case. The submitted 937 target includes `n=2` and uses lower inequalities for the exterior peaks, rather than asserting equality of all peaks.

These finite calculations support the interpretation of the displayed questions and expose a tension with informal historical summaries. They are a mathematical check, not a substitute for a formal statement bridge or independent review of the full theorems.
